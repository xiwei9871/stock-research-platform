# Wave E Frontier And Application Theme Research Design

Date: 2026-07-17
Status: approved

## Goal

Deliver five readable, evidence-backed Theme Research packages from the approved 82-chain Technology Industry Catalog, focusing Wave E on application-theme chains and frontier-technology chains that have strong current policy or industrial catalysts, meaningful A-share mapping potential, and clear value-chain questions.

Wave E must answer the user's practical questions for every theme:

- what the chain does and how value flows through it;
- which technical or commercial nodes control value capture;
- what observable catalysts could move the chain from research to orders or revenue;
- which listed companies are direct, elastic, indirect, or merely concept-associated beneficiaries;
- what first-party evidence supports each company relationship;
- what remains unproven, stale, or commercially immature.

The Technology Industry Catalog remains the structural directory. The full narrative, sources, claims, node assessments, company mappings, evidence gaps, and review state remain in Theme Research.

## Permanent Selection Invariant

All Wave E themes, and all later waves unless separately approved, must satisfy these rules:

- select an existing `chain_id` from the current approved 82-chain catalog;
- map one deep Theme Research record to exactly one catalog chain;
- do not create, rename, merge, or silently split catalog chains inside a research wave;
- preserve canonical ownership when an application or frontier theme depends on other chains;
- express cross-chain relationships through typed dependencies, node crosswalks, evidence references, and explicit exclusions;
- require a separate catalog-design decision before any 83rd chain is introduced.

Wave E is further limited to currently unselected `application_theme_chain` and `frontier_technology_chain` records. `ai_data_center_power` is not eligible because it is already covered by `ai_power_value_capture_v1`.

## Program Accounting

Theme counts use two explicit measures:

- Wave A through Wave D contain five themes each, so the wave program currently contains 20 themes.
- Five earlier foundation themes predate the A-D wave program.
- The current Theme Research priority pool therefore contains 25 themes in total: five foundation themes plus 20 Wave A-D themes.
- After Wave E is delivered, the A-E wave program will contain 25 themes and the total priority pool will contain 30 themes.

Wave readiness and total-pool readiness must be reported separately. A statement such as `Wave A-E 25/25` must not be presented as the total Theme Research count; the corresponding total-pool statement is `30/30`.

## Selection Rationale

Scheme A is preferred because it combines:

- two application chains with visible deployment, service, and operating-economics questions;
- three frontier chains with durable policy support and identifiable equipment, component, software, or service beneficiaries;
- strong adjacency to existing deep research without duplicating it;
- enough A-share candidates to build a useful company map while retaining strict evidence gates;
- distinct value-capture structures rather than five variations of the same semiconductor or equipment thesis.

The following eligible frontier chains are deferred:

| Deferred chain | Reason for deferral |
| --- | --- |
| `future_networks_next_generation_internet` | Boundary overlaps with 5G/6G, satellite applications, optical interconnect, and network equipment; canonical ownership and monetization need a narrower node design first |
| `spatial_computing_xr_metaverse_infrastructure` | Current A-share mappings are likely to mix components, mature consumer electronics, and concept exposure; commercialization evidence is less consistent than the selected five |
| `future_displays` | Requires a sharper separation between pre-commercial routes and the mature display-panel chain before beneficiary mappings can be reviewed consistently |
| `new_computing_routes` | The chain is intentionally broad across photonic, in-memory, neuromorphic, analog, biological, and other routes; one Wave E package would be too heterogeneous to produce a reliable beneficiary table |

Deferral is not rejection. These chains remain in the 82-chain catalog and may be selected in later waves after their evidence and boundary prerequisites are ready.

## Shared Deep-Research Contract

Every Wave E package must render the same seven readable sections used by the established industry-chain Theme Research contract.

### 1. Research conclusion

Provide a concise answer to:

- what the chain enables;
- its present technology and commercialization stage;
- the central bottleneck or value-capture conflict;
- the nodes most likely to receive orders, revenue, or strategic value;
- the strongest observable catalysts and risks;
- what remains unverified.

The conclusion must distinguish policy attention, scientific progress, engineering validation, procurement, delivered orders, recognized revenue, and recurring commercial demand.

### 2. Value-chain map

Render L3 stages as an ordered operating or technical flow. Each stage shows purpose, inputs, outputs, dependencies, key metrics, important L4 products or services, mapped companies, and evidence state.

For application themes, the map is an orchestration and service-delivery view over explicitly referenced canonical nodes. It must not copy or redefine the underlying hardware taxonomy.

For frontier themes, the map follows technical routes and validation stages. It must show where the chain moves from scientific apparatus to engineering prototype, qualification, delivery, and commercial operation.

### 3. Profit pools and barriers

Every important node explains why value may accumulate there. Accepted barrier types include qualification, scarce process know-how, proprietary algorithms, precision manufacturing, reliability, customer switching cost, regulation, long-duration service, standards position, installed base, and specialized capacity.

A high score without a sourced explanation is an evidence gap, not a conclusion.

### 4. Catalysts, validation signals, and risks

Each catalyst must name a measurable validation signal. Examples include pilot expansion, procurement announcement, device registration, clinical milestone, constellation utilization, service revenue, project delivery, test result, standard adoption, or repeat order.

Each risk must state what observation would weaken or invalidate the thesis. Policy inclusion alone cannot serve as both catalyst and proof of company benefit.

### 5. Company-beneficiary map

Use the existing four deterministic tiers:

- `核心受益`: direct product or service, reviewed evidence, and meaningful disclosed or strongly supported business exposure;
- `弹性受益`: direct exposure with limited current materiality but credible incremental sensitivity;
- `间接受益`: enabling equipment, materials, software, engineering, or services with a longer transmission path;
- `概念关联`: research participation, strategic intent, keyword association, undisclosed reserve, or unsupported market narrative.

Only the first three tiers appear in the default company table. Concept associations remain visible in a separate evidence-gap section.

Each reviewed company row must include:

- stock code and company name;
- application/frontier node and canonical owner node where applicable;
- disclosed product, service, project, customer, or qualification relationship;
- benefit-transmission path;
- business stage and materiality state;
- evidence publication and reporting dates;
- confidence, bottleneck relevance, review status, and unresolved gaps;
- direct row navigation to Stock Workspace, with no separate operation column.

### 6. Sources and evidence

Evidence priority remains:

1. annual/interim reports, exchange filings, official company product material, investor-relations records, and formal procurement or contract disclosures;
2. government, regulator, standards body, scientific facility, customer, supplier, industry association, or clinical-registry primary material;
3. identifiable industry and broker research;
4. reputable media used for corroboration or research leads;
5. social or oral claims retained only as unverified leads.

Frontier-company mappings require evidence of the company's specific role. Membership in a laboratory, alliance, strategic agreement, research project, or policy category is not sufficient by itself for reviewed-beneficiary status.

### 7. Evidence gaps and update history

Expose missing sources, outdated evidence, unquantified materiality, unresolved canonical ownership, unverified company relationships, immature technical milestones, and unmapped nodes. Preserve version, generation, review, and update history through the existing Theme Research model.

## Canonical Ownership Rules

### Application themes

- Physical products, components, and infrastructure remain mapped to their canonical catalog chains.
- Application pages aggregate those mappings through explicit chain/node references.
- A company may be mapped directly to an application node only when its disclosed relationship is application-specific, such as platform operation, data service, system orchestration, solution integration, or application delivery.
- The same product relationship must not be duplicated as two independently reviewed company mappings.

### Frontier themes

- Frontier-specific equipment, components, software, and services may be owned by the frontier theme when no existing canonical chain more precisely owns the relationship.
- Generic semiconductors, materials, power equipment, optical components, networks, medical devices, or industrial equipment retain their canonical ownership and are referenced as dependencies.
- Cross-chain aggregation must preserve the original evidence and materiality state.

## Theme-Specific Scope

### E1. Satellite Communications, Navigation, And Remote Sensing

Research flow:

1. orbital capacity and constellation service availability;
2. ground access, gateways, terminals, and network integration through canonical references;
3. satellite communications service delivery;
4. positioning, navigation, timing, and augmentation services;
5. remote-sensing data acquisition, processing, and distribution;
6. vertical applications in mobility, emergency response, agriculture, natural resources, mapping, maritime, and other evidenced scenarios;
7. service utilization, pricing, renewal, and operating economics.

Boundary with `satellite_manufacturing_space_infrastructure`:

- Wave D owns satellite buses, payload manufacturing, subsystems, integration, deployment hardware, and manufacturing evidence.
- Wave E owns downstream service delivery, data products, application economics, utilization, and application-specific integration.
- Hardware beneficiaries appear through canonical references unless the company supplies an application-specific terminal, platform, or service with distinct evidence.

Core research question: which parts of the satellite application stack can convert constellation buildout into measurable service demand and recurring revenue?

### E2. Intelligent Transport Vehicle-Road-Cloud

Research flow:

1. vehicle data and control interfaces through canonical vehicle-chain references;
2. roadside sensing, communication, and control infrastructure;
3. carrier and edge-network transport;
4. city or regional cloud platforms and data governance;
5. vehicle-road coordination, traffic-control, fleet, and mobility applications;
6. project construction, integration, operation, maintenance, and renewal;
7. deployment economics, utilization, safety validation, and public-service outcomes.

Boundary rules:

- vehicle hardware remains owned by vehicle architecture, intelligent driving, automotive electronics, and drive/chassis chains;
- roadside and network hardware remains owned by network and edge-IoT chains;
- general cloud infrastructure remains owned by the cloud data-center chain;
- Wave E owns cross-domain orchestration, application delivery, project economics, and application-specific platform or operator relationships.

Core research question: where does value accrue after pilot approval—hardware deployment, platform integration, data operations, application services, or recurring maintenance?

### E3. Brain-Computer Interfaces And Neural Engineering

Research flow:

1. invasive, minimally invasive, and non-invasive technical routes;
2. electrodes, neural sensors, biocompatible materials, and implantation interfaces;
3. signal acquisition, amplification, conversion, communication, and dedicated chips;
4. decoding, encoding, adaptive algorithms, operating systems, and software tools;
5. stimulation, feedback, rehabilitation, and assistive-control systems;
6. implantable and non-implantable devices;
7. surgical, clinical, industrial, rehabilitation, and consumer application paths;
8. testing, registration, clinical validation, manufacturing, and post-market evidence.

Boundary with `high_end_medical_devices` and mature medical chains:

- generic medical imaging, surgery equipment, and established diagnostic devices remain in their canonical chains;
- Wave E owns neural-interface-specific sensing, decoding, stimulation, integration, and validation;
- company mappings must separate approved products, clinical trials, research prototypes, and strategic plans.

The 2025 seven-department implementation opinion provides explicit 2027 and 2030 development targets, but policy targets must not be used as evidence of an individual company's product readiness or revenue.

Core research question: which technical routes and companies are progressing from laboratory validation toward repeatable products, registration, and commercially meaningful use?

### E4. Controlled Nuclear Fusion

Research flow:

1. magnetic-confinement, inertial-confinement, and alternative technical routes;
2. superconducting magnets, conductors, cryogenics, and structural systems;
3. high-power electrical supply, plasma heating, current drive, and control;
4. vacuum, pumping, gas handling, and tritium/fuel-cycle systems;
5. first wall, blanket, divertor, shielding, and advanced materials;
6. diagnostics, sensors, measurement, simulation, and control software;
7. precision manufacturing, installation, integration, testing, and maintenance;
8. project milestones from scientific experiment through engineering validation and demonstration facilities.

Boundary with `nuclear_power_equipment`:

- commercial fission equipment, fuel cycles, and plant operations remain in the nuclear-power chain;
- Wave E owns fusion-specific systems, qualification requirements, research facilities, engineering projects, and commercialization milestones;
- generic equipment suppliers appear only when filings identify a fusion-specific product, project, customer, qualification, or delivered order.

Core research question: which equipment and material nodes can realize orders during the long pre-commercial phase, and which claimed beneficiaries lack fusion-specific evidence?

### E5. Quantum Computing, Communication, And Measurement

Research flow:

1. quantum-computing routes and processor modalities;
2. control electronics, lasers, microwave systems, cryogenics, packaging, and test equipment;
3. software stacks, compilation, error correction, and cloud access;
4. quantum communication, key distribution, networking, and security integration;
5. quantum sensing, timing, navigation, metrology, and precision measurement;
6. standards, testing, deployment, service, and application validation;
7. scientific capability versus repeatable product, order, and service revenue.

Boundary rules:

- conventional network equipment remains in network and communication chains;
- general AI or classical compute hardware remains in compute-chip and infrastructure chains;
- generic cryogenic, optical, electronic, or test products require quantum-specific evidence before appearing as reviewed beneficiaries;
- computing, communication, and measurement claims must not be collapsed into one undifferentiated quantum label.

Core research question: which quantum sub-routes have observable procurement or service demand, and which A-share exposures are product-backed rather than policy or laboratory associations?

## Coverage And Review Gates

Wave E uses two completion levels.

### Readable delivery

A theme may enter the workspace as a useful `研究中` package when it has:

- all L3 stages described in an ordered flow;
- explicit canonical links and exclusions;
- at least ten accepted sources, including four primary or first-party sources;
- at least ten structured claims across value capture, catalysts, risks, barriers, and validation milestones;
- a research assessment or explicit evidence-gap state for every investment-relevant node;
- at least five reviewed A-share company mappings, or an explicit documented finding that the verified universe is smaller;
- concept-only companies separated from beneficiaries;
- readable evidence gaps and update history.

### Reviewed completion

A theme may display `已审核` only when it also satisfies the established full gate:

- every reviewed claim references accepted evidence;
- every reviewed company mapping has direct product/business evidence and a materiality state;
- at least eight reviewed company mappings are present when the chain supports that breadth;
- unsupported scores, stale evidence, and unresolved ownership conflicts are cleared or explicitly downgraded;
- deterministic beneficiary tiers match evidence, relationship, materiality, confidence, and bottleneck inputs;
- no concept association appears in the default beneficiary table.

Verified scarcity must never be solved by padding the table. If eight reviewed mappings are not supportable, the package remains readable but `研究中` until the gate is intentionally revised or stronger evidence is added.

## Product And Navigation Contract

Wave E reuses the existing Theme Research routes and page structure.

- All five records appear in `/theme-research` with their catalog identity and research status.
- Catalog list/detail surfaces show the linked theme, status, freshness, source count, reviewed-company count, evidence-gap count, and entry route.
- Theme pages provide conclusion, value-chain nodes, sources, company mapping, gaps, and update history through existing subroutes.
- Company tables have no separate operation column; clicking a company row opens Stock Workspace consistently with the review queue.
- Loading and incomplete-data states remain explicit rather than rendering empty sections.
- The catalog continues to expose all 82 chains regardless of deep-research status.

## Acceptance Criteria

Wave E is complete when evidence shows that:

- the catalog still contains exactly the approved 82 chains;
- the five selected existing `chain_id` values are registered without introducing or renaming a chain;
- all five Theme Research records load through the existing API and 5174 workspace;
- each page renders all required readable sections and explicit incomplete states;
- application themes aggregate canonical dependencies without duplicating company evidence;
- satellite applications do not duplicate satellite-manufacturing ownership;
- vehicle-road-cloud does not claim ownership of generic vehicle, roadside, network, or cloud products;
- frontier themes distinguish scientific participation, prototypes, qualifications, orders, revenue, and recurring demand;
- all reviewed claims and company mappings pass deterministic evidence validation;
- company tiers and row navigation behave consistently with the established Theme Research contract;
- concept-only candidates never appear as reviewed beneficiaries;
- targeted backend, frontend, database, build, and browser tests pass;
- desktop and mobile pages have no page-level horizontal overflow;
- the real authenticated application on port 5174 loads the five themes and their company rows open Stock Workspace.

## Non-Goals

- adding any chain outside the approved 82-chain catalog;
- completing the four deferred frontier chains in Wave E;
- treating a policy mention, research alliance, laboratory participation, strategic cooperation, patent, or keyword match as sufficient company-beneficiary evidence;
- copying canonical hardware taxonomies into application themes;
- presenting buy/sell recommendations, target prices, trading signals, or portfolio actions;
- forcing every theme to contain the same number of companies when evidence quality differs;
- replacing the existing Theme Research database, review, import, export, snapshot, or rollback workflows.
